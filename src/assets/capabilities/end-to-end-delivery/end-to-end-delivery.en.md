## One professional, connected responsibilities

Software engineering is my core discipline. Depending on the project, my contribution also includes understanding business needs, shaping solutions, coordinating testing and helping the team organise its work.

The umbrella illustrates how these areas connect. Software development forms the shaft and handle: my practical foundation. The canopy represents knowledge of neighbouring disciplines that helps me understand what other specialists need and how their work connects to mine. These are areas of knowledge and contribution, rather than a list of job titles I have held.

### The handle: software development

I work across the user interface, application logic and database access. Following a requirement through these layers helps me understand dependencies, investigate problems and decide where a change belongs.

### Within the team

I connect implementation with requirements, testing and documentation. I contribute directly to the code while helping developers and testers understand the purpose of their work and the dependencies between their tasks.

For example, a requirement describes the behaviour we need to build; the implementation determines which parts of the system change; testing checks the resulting behaviour. Working across these activities helps me keep the original need in view as the work progresses.

### Across the company

I help turn business needs into proposals that people can discuss and validate. I adapt technical explanations to different audiences and document decisions and processes so that the context remains available beyond the people who first created them.

This also supports continuity when someone joins a project or needs to understand an earlier decision. Documentation and onboarding are part of carrying the work forward.

### What holds the umbrella together

The value lies in the connections: requirements guide the code, implementation informs testing, and documentation preserves the context for future work. My foundation in development helps me relate those discussions to the practical work of building software.

The responsibilities I take on vary with the project. I aim to contribute where my experience is useful and work with the people whose knowledge complements mine.
